\section*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Preface}

This Constitution is founded upon a single governing principle: no person, institution, collective, nation, or other entity should ever hold arbitrary power over the survival or self-determination of another. All rights, structures, and limits in this document are derived from this principle.

Contributors should recognize that true freedom comes from the absence of domination -- the condition in which no other party holds the capacity to harm or arbitrarily coerce another at will. Formal rights without material security are hollow. Material security without genuine democratic participation is paternalism.

Contributors should acknowledge that this Constitution will reflect the material conditions and accumulated knowledge of its moment. Where pragmatic compromise is made -- including in the retention of market coordination among cooperatives -- it should be made transparently and with explicit invitation of revision as conditions change. Future generations may see failures within the initial design more clearly than the current stewards, and the Constitution is meant to handle continual analysis of material conditions and self-correction.
